Synthetic record — no real patient

It sets a generated discharge summary beside the thirty ward notes it was written from, and asks a clinician to decide, line by line, whether the record supports each statement. That means holding two columns of text side by side, a five-day chart and a thirty-record grid on screen at once. A phone cannot show them together, and reading them apart defeats the point.

Five steps, in order
  1. Read one line of the summary and click it. The evidence panel then shows only the records that bear on that line, and nothing else. On a wide screen the evidence sits beside the summary; on a narrow one it sits below it.
  2. Look at the evidence. Click any record in the list to see the exact row it came from, word for word, with the file name and row number. Nothing is paraphrased.
  3. Say what you think. Press ✓ if the line is fine, or ! if something is wrong with it — then type one line saying what. Every problem you mark appears on a list at the bottom of the screen, with the record that proves it, ready to send back.
  4. The researcher's reading is folded away. Wherever you see a dashed Show the researcher's reading button, there is a sentence written by hand for this demonstration saying what I make of that line. Nothing on this screen computed it. It is folded so you can form your own view first — open it whenever you want, or switch them all on at once with the button in the key bar.
  5. Deal with the leftovers in the strip at the bottom. Those are notes from the admission that no line of the summary uses. Either they did not need to be in the summary (press Accept) or something was left out (press Look at these).
